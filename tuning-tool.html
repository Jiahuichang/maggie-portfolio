E-BIKE TUNER · PRODUCT DESIGN

Turning technical parameters
into a shared tuning experience.

I worked with firmware and marketing to turn engineering knowledge into a mobile tuning tool—helping brands understand adjustments and take a more direct role in shaping ride feel.

Launched · iOS mobile appSee the turning point ↓
01 / OVERVIEW

Bring brands closer
to the tuning process.

Tuner lets engineers and brand customers configure the riding performance of e-bikes powered by Hyena drive systems. Customers can adjust ride feel in the app or use configurations provided by Hyena.

There was no dedicated Product Manager. As Product Designer, I helped define the requirements with firmware and marketing, translated technical knowledge into the experience, and developed the wireframes and UI.

03 / THE PROBLEM

Ride feel was getting lost
between teams.

Brands described how they wanted a bike to feel. Those requests passed through sales and other teams before reaching firmware engineers. Each round of clarification and adjustment added another handoff.

1 month or more

of back-and-forth communication to get tuning adjustments right in the previous process.

Engineers also relied on laptop-based tools for on-site tuning. When remote exchanges could not resolve the request, travel added further time and cost.

04 / THE TURNING POINT

A familiar partner.
An untested assumption.

We started with a well-known US brand that had worked with Hyena for years. I initially assumed its engineers understood what each parameter controlled and how an adjustment would change the ride.

INITIAL ASSUMPTION

Give engineers access to the parameters.

Long-standing collaboration seemed to imply a shared understanding of the tuning controls.

WHAT WE LEARNED

Understanding and comparison mattered.

The customer's engineers did not fully understand each parameter's effects. They also wanted to save parameter sets and compare different riding configurations.

This shifted the design focus toward explaining adjustments and supporting configuration comparison.

05 / DESIGN DECISIONS

Connect engineering knowledge
with the customer's task.

01

Understand the parameters before designing the controls

I interviewed firmware engineers about their past tuning work and actual operating practices. Together, we clarified what each parameter meant, what it affected, and its adjustable range. This gave me a technical basis for defining the requirements and designing the experience.

02

Design around riding configurations

The customer's need extended beyond editing individual values: they wanted to retain parameter sets and compare riding configurations. I brought this need into the product requirements, so the design addressed how customers evaluate ride feel across different setups.

03

Support both customer adjustments and supplied configurations

Tuner gives brands two ways to explore ride feel: adjust it themselves or use a configuration provided by Hyena. This also gives sales a practical way to introduce the riding experience when working with prospective brand customers.

07 / REFLECTION

Shared history does not guarantee
shared understanding.

This project changed how I approach expert tools. Even engineers at a long-standing partner may need help connecting a technical parameter with its practical effect.

My value was in uncovering that gap, learning the tuning domain from firmware engineers, and translating it into requirements and designs the teams could develop together.
