A product built around
the everyday ride.

My task was to turn those possibilities into a focused first release that the team could deliver, measure, and improve.

03 / CHALLENGE & STRATEGY

More possibilities.
Less capacity to pursue them.

01

A scattered vision

Ride tracking, gamification, planting, community, rewards, and future e-bike features competed for attention.

02

An unclear reason to switch

Existing HRA riders did not immediately understand why they should move to Flex.

03

A shrinking team

As team members left, the original roadmap no longer matched our development capacity.

The question became: what must work in the first release for riders to experience real value?

Make the ride valuable first.

I centered the first release on a clear value loop. Engagement features needed to reinforce the ride experience, not compete with it.

  1. 01

    Record

    Capture a daily ride.

  2. 02

    Visualize

    Revisit the journey.

  3. 03

    Progress

    See effort add up.

  4. 04

    Return

    Find a reason to ride again.

Core product loop · A simplified view of the strategy.

Define a release boundary.

I weighed rider value, dependency risk, implementation effort, and whether a feature was necessary for the first usable experience. When capacity changed, I revisited the scope—not just the dates.

FIRST RELEASE

Protect the core experience

  • Ride recording, GPS maps & 3D ride records
  • Reports, weekly challenges & achievements
  • Rewards & planting
  • Feed, community, profile & notifications

DEFERRED / FUTURE SCOPE

Keep expansion off the critical path

  • Strava & HealthKit integrations
  • Expanded e-bike connectivity & status
  • Automatic ride start / finish & multi-bike support
  • Streaks, leaderboards, more languages & Android
  • Watch support & Live Activities

Scope summary, not a numerical scoring model. Deferred work is not presented as shipped functionality.

05 / DECISIONS & EXECUTION

The trade-offs that
made a release possible.

01

Core ride value before ecosystem expansion

Translated the ride-first strategy into PRDs, user flows, and feature specifications, giving product and engineering a shared definition of the first release.

02

Rebuild the roadmap around actual capacity

Re-scoped milestones and dependencies as resources decreased, then aligned release scopes, QA preparation, and test checklists with the plan the team could execute.

03

Measure the journey beyond sign-up

Defined events and tracking plans around starting and completing rides so we could examine experienced value, not only account creation.

04

Align the experience from onboarding to launch

Coordinated onboarding copy, achievement messaging, notification scenarios, support materials, policy pages, and launch messaging so they reflected the same product behavior.

06 / ITERATIONS & IMPACT

A shipped product.
